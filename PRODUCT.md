# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individuals who want a focused, distraction-free way to plan their week. They are not project managers, not part of a team using a shared workspace as their primary workflow — they are people who want to see their week at a glance and get things done. They use this alongside life, not instead of it: the tool should feel like a well-made personal object, not a productivity platform they have to maintain.

## Product Purpose

A weekly planner that keeps the week visible, tasks within reach, and everything else out of the way. Success looks like: a user opens the app, sees their week clearly, adds a task in two seconds, marks it done, and closes the tab — with no friction and no management overhead.

## Brand Personality

Warm, focused, human. Approachable without being playful. Quiet without being cold. The app feels like it belongs to the person using it — personal in the way a well-worn notebook is personal, not in the way a fitness app sends you push notifications.

## Anti-references

- **Not Notion**: no document/wiki aesthetic, no blocks, no slash commands, no page-within-page nesting. This is not a second brain.
- **Not enterprise PM tools (Asana, Linear, Jira, Monday)**: no ticket numbers, priority matrices, sprint boards, status badges, or "project health" dashboards. Tasks are not issues.
- **Not generic SaaS**: the gray-sidebar + blue-accent + identical-card-grid template that every B2B tool uses. This is a personal tool, not a product for a company's productivity stack.

## Design Principles

1. **Week as the unit of meaning** — the 7-column grid is the core surface; everything else (month view, teams, categories) serves it without competing with it.
2. **Personal over generic** — choices in type, color, and spacing should feel like they belong to this specific app, not like they were assembled from a dashboard component library.
3. **Clarity without coldness** — minimal, but warmth is carried through craft: considered spacing, type that breathes, a palette that has a point of view.
4. **Tasks, not tickets** — no status theater. A task belongs to a day, gets done or doesn't, and that's the whole model.
5. **Frictionless capture** — adding a task should feel instantaneous. The UI should never ask the user to do more than the task requires.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target, but sensibly accessible: sufficient text contrast, keyboard navigation, and meaningful screen-reader semantics as a baseline. Color-coded categories should never be the only indicator.
